1. Purpose and objective

Milestone 2 delivers the Test Solution's independent capability to validate AI-generated test artifacts for both ATL105 and ISO8583. The Test Solution will derive its own source-anchored rule baseline and assess AI-generated business requirements (BRs), test scenarios (TSs), test cases (TCs), test data (TD), traceability, and coverage.

Every result must be evidence-backed and reported as passed, failed, review-required, or blocked. Unresolved interpretations are not silently treated as valid or invalid.

Specification baselines: BUYPASS ATL105 2026-3; First Data ISO8583 Global Version 2026-3; and Global TA Addendum Version 2021-1a, subject to an applicability and precedence decision.

2. Scope

In scope

  • Inventory, identify, and hash specification versions and relevant sections, appendices, tables, message/segment definitions, and addenda.
  • Independently derive source-anchored Test Solution rules and BRs, distinct from AI-generated BRs and AIV-* validator controls.
  • Build or extend reusable intake, normalization, schema, traceability, coverage, semantic-validation, reporting, and regression-test capabilities.
  • Create specification-specific rule packs for agreed ATL105 and ISO8583 scope.
  • Validate frozen AI deliveries, retaining provenance, hashes, results, discrepancies, and unresolved questions.
  • Manually review a risk-stratified 5-10% sample of eligible AI-generated end-to-end flows.
  • Retest defects and publish results, limitations, and required decisions.

Out of scope

  • Generating or correcting the AI Solution's BRs, scenarios, cases, or data on its behalf.
  • Claiming host, network, processor, or production execution from offline validation.
  • Treating structural links, placeholders, AI-reported confidence/coverage, or the manual sample as proof of approval or complete correctness.
  • Promoting unresolved source conflicts or business interpretations without an authorized decision.
  • Expanding either specification beyond scope agreed at the 6 November scope gate.

4. Four-resource plan

All four test resources (R1-R4) are cross-trained and capable of performing every activity: specification understanding, independent Test Solution BR/rule authoring, automation and validation, and manual review of AI-generated flows. Resource labels do not denote fixed specialist roles.

At kickoff, assign a rotating activity lead for each work package and a different peer reviewer for its evidence. Rotate leads across ATL105 and ISO8583 and across analysis, authoring, automation, and review as capacity permits. All resources participate in planning, implementation, cross-review, and final evidence review. Avoid relying on one person as the sole author or reviewer of an oracle, validator control, or acceptance result.

Collaboration, independence, and rotation

  • The assigned rule author derives rules from specification evidence before comparing with AI artifacts. A different resource reviews source interpretation and evidence links.
  • The assigned automation author implements controls from source-supported or explicitly bounded rules; a different resource reviews the control and regression evidence, including what it does not prove.
  • The assigned manual sample lead selects flows only after AI delivery freeze. Reviewers judge AI content against the independently derived Test Solution oracle, never using AI content to author that oracle.
  • Rotate lead and reviewer assignments between work packages and, where practical, between specifications. Record names, roles, and review evidence in the work register.
  • Unresolved semantics go to an authorized SME/TBA or delegate. Cross-training does not grant business-approval authority; no resource may represent an unapproved assumption as an approval.

6. Manual end-to-end AI-flow review

Manual review is independent quality evidence, not a substitute for automated coverage or a claim of statistical confidence.

  1. Freeze the population. R4 records delivery ID, specification/version, file hashes, intake time, and exclusions. Eligible flows trace from an AI-generated BR or declared source requirement through scenario and case to associated test data. Keep incomplete/orphaned flows in the defect population and report them.
  2. Set the denominator. Count unique eligible end-to-end flows after deduplicating exact duplicates within the frozen delivery. Report separately per specification and in-scope message/segment or flow family. List excluded/incomplete flows and reasons.
  3. Target 7.5% per specification. For each specification with at least 10 eligible unique flows, select a count within 5-10%: start at ceil(0.075 x eligible flows), cap at floor(0.10 x eligible flows), and ensure at least ceil(0.05 x eligible flows). If rounding makes the band impossible, seek Test Lead approval for an exception before review. For fewer than 10 flows, approve an exception or block the gate. Zero/unknown denominator is blocked, never 100%.
  4. Stratify by risk and coverage. Include both specifications and, where present, high-impact transaction/message families, conditional rules, positive/negative and boundary cases, and source/version changes. Prioritize ambiguous claims, complex links, and high-risk data. Record the selection method, seed/deterministic rule, and per-stratum counts.
  5. Protect the cap. If minimum stratum representation would exceed 10%, seek Test Lead approval for a revised sample or report uncovered strata as a limitation. Do not silently exceed the cap.
  6. Review independently. Compare applicability, BR meaning, source anchor, scenario, expected result, test data, and traceability against the Test Solution oracle and specification. Record reviewer, evidence, result, defect severity, and SME adjudication need.
  7. Report honestly. Publish numerator, denominator, percentage, selection method, distribution, findings, and limitations. The sample does not prove that unreviewed AI flows are correct.

7. Validator capabilities and acceptance criteria

Acceptance is against documented scope. A validator pass means the checked control passed; it does not universally approve the AI package.

Common required capabilities

  • Intake and provenance: Preserve original delivery; identify producer/package/version, specification/version, available generation metadata, and source hashes; fail or explicitly hold unsupported formats.
  • Canonicalization and schema: Map supported formats without silent information loss; validate structure, types, IDs, and specification-specific payload shape.
  • Independent oracle: Keep source-derived Test Solution rules/BRs separate from AI output; every in-scope rule has source evidence and status.
  • Traceability: Validate rule/BR-to-scenario-to-case-to-data links, IDs, duplicates, orphans, unresolved references, and required reciprocal links.
  • Coverage: Recompute coverage from eligible artifacts and the independent denominator; compare producer claims; do not credit placeholders or heuristic matches.
  • Specification checks: Run only source-backed implemented checks; distinguish bounded format/shape predicates from full semantic, lifecycle, host, or business validation.
  • Review state: Emit explicit pass/fail/review-required/blocked results; preserve conflicts, missing evidence, and SME dependencies.
  • Evidence and regression: Produce deterministic, versioned reports and tests for valid controls, targeted invalid mutations, boundary cases, and unresolved cases.

ATL105 exit

  • Source/version, rule denominator, chain scope, and report snapshots reconciled or explicitly bounded by approved scope.
  • Every in-scope rule has stable source anchor/status; gaps and SME dependencies visible.
  • Agreed AI delivery ingested and assessed by common validator and ATL105 rule pack.
  • Structural, traceability, supported semantic, and coverage controls have regression evidence; placeholders/review-only evidence are distinguished.
  • Manual sample completed, or gate explicitly blocked with owner/reason.

ISO8583 exit

  • Both source files inventoried and hash-identified; applicability, relationship, precedence, and contradictions documented.
  • Agreed scope has independent source-anchored rules/BRs with applicability and open questions recorded.
  • Versioned ISO8583 pack accepted by shared validator for agreed AI format/scope.
  • Automated structural, traceability, coverage, and source-supported semantic checks have regression evidence, including defined positive/negative/boundary mutations.
  • AI delivery, manual sample, limitations, and SME dependencies reported.

Milestone exit by 15 Dec

  • Both scopes have dated, hash-backed baselines and agreed denominators or explicit NOT_CALCULABLE limitations.
  • Agreed controls execute reproducibly; no in-scope artifact is silently dropped or converted to success.
  • Failures, unresolved semantics, unsupported formats, and approval dependencies are assigned; unresolved items are never promoted to pass.
  • Automated regression suites pass, or remaining failures have impact, owner, and acceptance decision documented.
  • Manual review population and results are auditable.
  • Test Lead and authorized stakeholders record per-spec acceptance, conditional acceptance, or blocked status.

8. Governance, reporting, and change control

  • Daily: Update the shared task/decision/defect register with status, evidence link, next action, and blocker.
  • Weekly: Test Lead reviews scope, schedule exits, control/test results, sample readiness, SME decisions, and risks; publish a concise status per specification.
  • Change control: Record any scope, source-version, denominator, or sampling-method change with reason, impact, approver, and baseline revision; preserve prior evidence.
  • Defects: Separate AI artifact defects, validator defects, and source/SME questions. Correct validator logic when wrong; do not edit frozen AI input during assessment.
  • Release decision: Authorized stakeholders accept documented scope and limitations. Technical completion alone does not authorize business semantics or production use.

10. Decisions required at kickoff

  1. Name the four cross-trained resources, Test Lead, AI delivery contacts, and authorized SME/TBA or delegate for each specification; agree the rotating lead/reviewer assignment method.
  2. Confirm full ISO8583 scope or a bounded prioritized subset. If full scope is required, confirm expected AI package, message/transaction inventory, and acceptance denominator by 6 November.
  3. Confirm the effective relationship and precedence of ISO8583 Global TA Addendum 2021-1a relative to Global Version 2026-3.
  4. Confirm AI artifact formats, frozen delivery dates, and expected end-to-end flow structure for both specifications.
  5. Approve manual sampling policy: 7.5% target, 5% floor, 10% cap, and escalation where risk-stratum representation cannot fit within the cap.
  6. Approve acceptance vocabulary and authorize the Test Lead to report CONDITIONAL or BLOCKED rather than assert completion when decisions, inputs, or evidence are missing.
